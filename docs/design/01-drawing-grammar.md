# Drawing Grammar · `DG`

What a mark may be. These rules govern the geometry itself — the stroke, the fill that is never allowed, the taper, the wander, the grain. They apply identically to the map, the figures, the chapter openers and the seeds, because all four are one drawing made four times.

## DG-01 — Never a Straight Line

Nothing in the root/neural/light system is ever drawn as a perfectly straight segment — no `<line>` elements, no `L`-only paths, no zero-bow filaments. Living tissue does not do that. Use `smoothPolyline()` for traced paths and `arcSegment()` wherever a short straight stroke would otherwise appear; `Filament` enforces a minimum curvature automatically. Every line is slightly electric, elegant, neuronal: roots and dendrites, neurons and electric pulses. There is **no exception inside the drawing**. There used to be a second one — the background data-grid, excused as technical backdrop rather than tissue — and it was removed along with the grid itself (**The Ground Is Black, and Nothing Is Drawn On It**).

**The scope is the drawing, and four interface glyphs sit outside it.** `ChevronLeft`, `ChevronRight`, `ArrowLeft` and `ArrowRight` in `organic/Icons.tsx` are straight, by request: they are pictograms on controls, they grow out of nothing, carry no conduction and take no tide, and at 12–20px the 2–4% bow that satisfies this rule everywhere else reads as a wobble on a button rather than as a pointer. The exemption is enforced BY SYMBOL, not by file — `audit-lines` releases those four names and still fails a straight stroke anywhere else in the same file, including in the nineteen other glyphs. Every other mark in the icon set is an arc. See docs/OPEN.md 49.

## DG-02 — Only Lines. Nothing Is Ever a Filled Area

No element in any drawing — map, figure, opener, seed — may be a filled shape or a soft gradient disc. Flat tone over an area reads as a mass at *any* opacity, and a mass is a blob; this was learned four separate times (halo discs at 0.4, soma membranes at 0.17, then at 0.055, bouton and relay-cell haloes at 0.28) and the fix was never a lower number, it was removing the fill. A cell body is spoken by **line density**: a pappus of filaments crowding at the receptacle, an arbor coning outward, fibres landing on it. Points are not areas — a bouton, a seed tip, a nucleolus is a bright dot and is allowed — the nucleolus sits off-axis, as it does in life, and the relay cell at a crossing is drawn as its own outline in filament, never as a filled spindle. The only fills left in the system are the **light sources**: the central emitter and the chapter beacons — both layered radial falloffs, bright at the heart, breathing on the tide. A light source is layered and BRIGHT; the grey halos that were removed were single dim discs, and the difference between haze and lamp is intensity, not construction. Chapter beacons carry no solid hot dot at the very centre (the numeral lives there, white on white). Never reintroduce a dim fill to "give the shape presence".

## DG-03 — A Lamp Burns Where the Tissue Is Thick

This extends **Only Lines. Nothing Is Ever a Filled Area** and adds no fill anywhere. The light sources that rule permits — the central emitter and the chapter beacons — each take one intensity whatever they sit in, so a cell carrying eight fibres burns exactly as bright as one carrying three, and the light reads as a lamp placed behind the drawing. In the references it is never that: the form itself glows, hottest where the material is thickest or turns toward the viewer, dark where it thins. This is **A Sphere Is Where the Fibres Come From** carried into intensity: cell body, flow and fibre are already required to agree about where the connections leave, and brightness is one more thing that has to agree with them. So a beacon's inner stops are keyed to the structure at its own node — degree, arbor density, soma radius — computed once at module scope from the graph that already exists. The cost is zero: the same circles, different stop values, no per-frame work and no new elements. Three guards, because this must not become a licence. **It modulates only the fills already sanctioned** — never grounds to give some other element a glow, and **A Cell That Emits No Light Takes No Temperature** still holds, so the chapter opener gets nothing. **There is a floor, and under it the fill is removed rather than dimmed**: if keying by density would take a beacon below lamp intensity it draws no fill at all, because the difference between haze and lamp is intensity, and a dim disc is precisely the blob that was removed four times — the fix was never a lower number. And the peak stays under the saturation ceiling in **Black, White, and One Temperature of Light**.

## DG-04 — Fibres Take Direct Courses

Wander amplitude is ~5% of span with one dominant low frequency and one small secondary — never more. A fibre wandering by a fifth of its length with a wobble running thirteen cycles across the span is a scribble, not tissue: real axons are under tension and are going somewhere. Because amplitude scales with length, over-wandering hits hardest exactly where there is room to loop, and every loop crosses three other strands — which is what made the map read as chaotic and unreal.

## DG-05 — Hierarchy Is Generations, Not a Ratio

The taper went from a flat 2.5:1 to roughly 5:1 because at 2.5:1 every line read alike. The reference goes much further — the plasmodium runs about **30:1** from its fattest trunk down to reticulations barely above the noise floor. But the ratio is not what makes it legible: what does is that each generation is *separably* wider than the next, so the levels can be counted at a glance. A 30:1 span reached by two extremes with a smooth ramp between them reads as a gradient, which is the same failure as a flat width wearing different clothes. Aim for **at least four visibly distinct generations**, each a clear step from its parent, and the step has to survive at reading scale rather than only in the geometry. **Buy the extra range at the thin end.** Fattening trunks is the obvious move and it is the wrong one — it fights **Legibility Comes From the Ground, Not From Thicker Strands** and turns a process back into a stroke. Hold the trunk where it is and push the floor down, which is also what the reference does: its main veins are not enormous, its finest ones are almost nothing.

## DG-06 — Density Is the Texture

The references this drawing answers to get their material richness from thousands of small marks reading as one surface. The line-only equivalent is beading — more, finer varicosities on the two finest dendrite generations, sized so the eye reads grain at a glance and individual beads only on approach. The keep rate is 0.46 and the radius factor 0.6; both are tuned against element count, not taste. **Measure before changing them**: the map's baseline is 7,564 SVG elements and the current density takes it to 8,057 (+6.5%); a figure like FIG 0.1 goes 2,525 → 2,729 (+8.1%). The "~6,000 elements" figure quoted in older comments is stale and was already exceeded before this change. Revert by returning the keep rate to 0.34 if a measured frame budget ever says so.

## DG-07 — A Sphere Is Where the Fibres Come From

A vortex whose every streamline is clamped inside its own radius is an ornament parked on the network, and a circular `clipPath` — however wide — cuts each strand along one perfect arc, which is a boundary. `VortexSphere` takes `exits`: the true angles of the fibres leaving that cell (`fibreAngles()`, the same list the soma silhouette cones along). A few streamlines escape along them, spiralling out of the flow field and straightening onto the fibre, tapered over three chained segments so they dissolve into the arbor. Cell body, flow and fibre must always agree about where the connections leave. The efferents render **outside** the rotating bands — a strand that leaves along an axon and then rotates off it is worse than no strand.

## DG-08 — A Field Is Rays; A Cell Is an Arbor

These are different shapes for different jobs and they are not interchangeable. `growDendrite` curls every segment by ±0.35 rad and forks by ±0.42–0.72, so over three or four generations the outward bearing **randomises completely** — which is correct for a cell, whose branches gather locally, and catastrophic for a field, which has to hold its bearing to say *outward*. FIG 0.1 was first built with the field as one enormous arbor (20 arms, depth 4, radius 520) and rendered as uniform speckle with a knot in the middle: 2.5% ink coverage, flat in every direction, no radial read at all. No amount of tuning `fadeR` fixes it, because the failure is the shape. A field is drawn instead as **long rays that keep their heading** — each wandering, since nothing here is ever straight, but by a slow bearing drift scaled by distance so the near end stays honestly radial and only the far end strays. Chain each ray in segments so it thins and dims along its length, exactly as `tissue()` tapers, and space the rays on the golden angle: an even fan reads as a sunburst, and a sunburst is a graphic device, not a space. Verify with a radial coverage profile — it must fall smoothly and then **plateau** with ink still arriving at the frame's corners (measured after the rebuild: 28% → 13% → 8% → 5% → 3% → ~1%, all four corners lit). A profile that collapses to near-zero inside the frame is a small drawing with a soft edge, not a horizon.

## DG-09 — An Unbounded Field Fades; It Never Ends

Where a drawing has to say *this space does not stop*, it may not draw a ring, a rim or a horizon line — a boundary around media makes media a container, and a container is the opposite of the claim. The mechanism is the ray field (see **A Field Is Rays; A Cell Is an Arbor**): rays chained in segments that thin and dim as `(1-t)^0.85` and run past the frame corner, so the sheet crops something already dissolving. An earlier per-arbor fade (`fadeR`/`arborFade`, a radial opacity falloff inside `growDendrite`) was retired with the ray rebuild — it produced fog, and once nothing called it the machinery was removed rather than left as a loaded gun. Do not reintroduce a fade into the arbor generator; fading is a property of a field, and fields are rays.
