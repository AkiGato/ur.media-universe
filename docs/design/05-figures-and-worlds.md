# Figures and Worlds · `FW`

The five data visualisations, the surfaces they open onto, and the arguments they are required to make. A figure is the map at figure scale — never a bespoke composition, never a shrunken diagram, never a panel over a page.

[← index](00-index.md)

## FW-01 — Every Figure Is the Same Organism as the Map

All five data visualisations are `LivingFigure` (`src/components/figures/LivingFigure.tsx`) — the Orrery's grammar at figure scale. A figure declares only cells (`FigureNode`) and, optionally, which cells connect; the courses, the crossings, the polarity, the phases and the propagation are all derived. Connections are single tapering processes drawn by the same `tissue()` routine the map uses — trunk thinning to twig, varicosities along the way, one bright terminal where it lands — crossings fuse through a bipolar relay cell drawn as an outline, and the luminance tide takes its phase from distance to the figure's core. When the map's grammar moves, port it: LivingFigure was left a full generation behind once (bundles, myelin, hillocks, filled membranes, the old curve) and the reader quietly stopped looking like the thing it opens out of. Figures are touched, not read: hovering a cell propagates excitation outward by *graph* distance (never geometry), and pressing one pins its reading into the strip under the frame. Never go back to drawing a figure as a bespoke composition of flowers and labels — it looked adjacent to the map instead of being made of it. The same holds for the chapter cells (`ChapterOpener.tsx`): the figure engine, the openers and the map are one drawing made three times, not three drawings that resemble each other. Anatomy the map drops *for scale reasons* may legitimately stay where the scale differs — the opener keeps its myelin because it draws one axon at roughly a hundred times map size, where every internode reads.

## FW-02 — Figures Are Filaments, Never Boxes

All data visualisations are built from `src/components/figures/FigurePrimitives.tsx` — fine luminous filaments, glowing cores, seed tips and roots on empty black. Never use rectangles, flowchart boxes, or arrowheads in a figure. The symbolic vocabulary is one parametric generator: `Burst` (dandelion, `scatter` 0→1 disperses the seed head), `Lotus` (`openness` 0→1, `petalIntensity` per petal), `RootFan`, `Core`, `Halo`, `Filament`. (`DataTicks` was in this list until nothing drew one any more — scattered arc ticks read as tissue behind tissue, which is what retired them from the map ground — and an unused generator is a loaded gun, so it was removed rather than kept.) Geometry is deterministic (seeded `rnd`), monochrome, and uses `currentColor` so both themes work. Wrap every figure in `FigureFrame`.

## FW-03 — A Figure Is a World, and a World Has Three Passages

A figure opened on its own surface is not a panel over a page — it is a place, and it carries no close cross, because a cross says "this is a window on something else". It has three passages instead, and each says *where it goes* rather than what it does to the window: **the chapter** that argues it, **the map** that holds every chapter, and **the practice** — the Production Ruleset rule that puts it to work, which is the only exit that is not more reading. The mapping lives in one place, `FIGURE_PASSAGES` in `pageModel.ts`, and it is not chosen loosely: each rule already cites its own figure in its text (branch-2 names "the Causal Taxonomy (Ch. IV)", branch-1 names "the three cognitive postures (Ch. 3.3)"). A passage is drawn as a soma and a name, the same two marks the map uses for everything it offers, so an exit from the tissue looks like tissue rather than like a button bolted to it; the forward passage is marked by light (`.bud-lit`), never by weight or a box. Esc still returns to the page, which is a fourth way out and deliberately the quietest.

## FW-04 — A World Carries Less Text Than the Page It Came From

Inside `FigureWorldContext` the whole `FigureFrame` steps aside — no membrane, no caption, no tag, no veins, no foot — because all four restate on the surface what the surface already says at its top edge. The context exists rather than a prop because the five diagram components sit between the stage and the frame and none of them has any business knowing about layout. And inside a world the figures finally obey **Labels Surface Under Touch**, which they used to break by standing every name and every sub-line open at rest: a name now surfaces where you touch and warms outward by topological distance (measured: touched 0.95, one hop 0.30, two hops 0.15), the second line belongs to the touched cell alone, and the caption strip carries its reading. The **glyph exception stands** — a magnitude that is the figure's whole argument (`7±2`, `∞`) stays legible at rest, exactly as the map's chapter numerals do, or the reader is left with cells of identical construction and no way to tell them apart but pointing at each in turn.

## FW-05 — A Figure Has a Far Plane

Cells are drawn in two passes, not one. Field cells — the `kind: 'minor'` swarms that exist to say *there are more of these than you can count* — recede behind one Gaussian pass (`-recede`, stdDeviation 0.85 user units) at 0.72 opacity, so the cells carrying the argument are the only sharp things on the sheet. Three constraints, all load-bearing: the blur is applied to the **group**, never per cell, because one filter pass over one rasterised group is cheap and twenty-six are not; a cell recedes **only if it carries no name**, so text can never be blurred by construction rather than by anyone remembering; and the original array index is carried through the partition, because every period, phase and seed here derives from a cell's position in `nodes` and re-indexing redraws the whole organism. Keep the blur under one user unit — past that the arbors fuse into the grey mass every other rule in this file exists to avoid. **No parallax on the far plane**: edges are drawn in an earlier pass and would not follow the transform, so the cells would detach from their own fibres.

## FW-06 — Never a Shrunken Figure

The map's rule applies to every figure. A figure measures the *column* it was handed (a `ResizeObserver` on a wrapper that is the same DOM node in both modes — branch on a value, never on an early return, or the observed node unmounts and the figure can never switch back) and below 600px it renders a legible stacked spine of its cells instead of a scaled-down drawing: soma, name, sub, and on press the reading, the detail and the `Wired to ·` list, so the topology survives the loss of the picture. Never restore a `min-w` on a figure SVG — that trades an unreadable diagram for a side-scrolling one.

## FW-07 — A Schematic Takes the Whole Sheet

`BookSpread` groups pages once and pages group-by-group; it does not derive the pair from the parity of the index. Parity forces every page into a pair, and a figure in half a spread is a figure at half scale. Diagram pages (and the cover) are their own group, and diagram pages drop the reading margin (`p-2 sm:p-3`, `max-w-5xl`) because the margin that makes prose comfortable is the width a figure needs. Grouping is also what keeps navigation honest — no page is skipped because its partner rendered alone.

## FW-08 — A Chapter Opens As Its Cell

The first page of a chapter carries that chapter's soma grown large — membrane, vortex, dendritic field, the numeral surfacing from inside it — because scale is the difference between arriving somewhere and scrolling past it. Rendered on the chapter's existing first page, never as a new page: bookmarks, highlights and the saved position are all stored by index, and five moments of scale are not worth silently moving a reader's marks.

## FW-09 — A List Is Not a Cause

FIG 3.1 drew the taxonomy as three parallel columns under a headline — economic, historical, neuro-psychological, equal and side by side. That is how Chapter IV first names them and it is weaker than what the chapter goes on to say: economic and historical conditions are **external**, and the neuro-psychological register is **internal** — "the cognitive and emotional architecture that the story is landing inside". Two causes and one product are not three peers. The figure is now a causal order: the two external roots converge (a single dense cell, because a convergence is a condition, not a junction), the convergence passes through the reader's own position as a **relay** rather than a fourth cause, and the response comes out the other side. Read down it is causal; read up — the direction a reader actually arrives, at the headline — it is the deconstruction the chapter asks for. **The prose in 4.2–4.5 still calls the three roots "a triangulated reading" and now disagrees with the figure; that text needs updating.**

## FW-10 — Every Cell in an Observer-Relative Figure Is a Centre

If a figure claims there is no absolute frame, the drawing must not quietly keep one. Network cells carry their own `arborR`/`arborFade` fields — the same drawing at a smaller scale — because a satellite with no field of its own would restate the centre as privileged and undo the argument. Nothing is concentric and nothing is evenly spaced: cells sit at unequal radii and unequal angles so each is struck by a different part of the field from a different bearing. **The distortion is the geometry**, not an effect laid over it — which is what makes it a distortion of a perception that was already subjective before the network reached it.
