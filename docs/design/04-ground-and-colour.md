# The Ground and Its Colour · `GR`

What lies behind the drawing, and what colour anything is allowed to be. The ground is the app's main contrast instrument: nearly every legibility problem here was solved by emptying the background rather than by touching the drawing.

[← index](00-index.md)

---

## GR-01 — The Ground Is Black, and Nothing Is Drawn On It

Every drawing surface is black — the map, the figure stage, the instrument stage, a chapter's cell — in **both** themes. Tissue here is emitted light, and emitted light on white is just ink: the glow, the tide, the beacons and the `-core` stops all describe something luminous against a void, and on a white ground every one of them inverts into a smudge. The reader's prose pages keep the light/dark preference because prose is ink and ink is happy either way; the drawings do not have a preference, and `isDark` must never reach a surface whose job is to hold tissue. And nothing is drawn on that ground: no grid, no rule, no field, no ticks, no technical backdrop. The map arrived here by attrition — the grid was dimmed to 0.14 to stop it competing and then removed outright, on the finding that a rectilinear field behind a thing containing no straight lines is the one texture guaranteed to read as *diagram* rather than as organism. Figures inherited that grid and kept it longer; they get the same answer. This is also what makes **Never a Straight Line** absolute: the exception it used to carve out was the backdrop grid, and there is no longer a backdrop to except. The empty ground is not minimalism, it is contrast — see **Legibility Comes From the Ground, Not From Thicker Strands**, of which this is the end state.

## GR-02 — Plain Ground — the Glass Is Retired

There is no glassmorphism. Every gradient field, backdrop blur and feathered light-well is switched off in the PLAIN GROUND block at the end of `src/index.css`, deliberately last so it wins over everything above it. Black is black, white is white, and the only things on the ground are type and tissue — the same ground the map has always had. Surfaces that genuinely sit *over* content (drawers, the search well, the highlight popover, the chrome bands) are solid theme ground, never translucent, so what is beneath can never bleed through the words they carry. Everything that merely decorated a page gets nothing. The `.glass-*` classes survive in markup as layout hooks only and draw nothing.

## GR-03 — The Map's Ground Is Empty

Nothing behind the organism may be made of filaments. The ambience layer's drifting data flowers and the scattered arc `DataTicks` are the same mark the tissue is drawn with, so at rest the eye could not separate backdrop from anatomy and the connections read as one more layer of texture — the map looked busy and said nothing. That layer is now gone from the whole app rather than only from the map. `GlassAmbience` drew those flowers and a rectilinear CSS grid behind the *reader*, on the argument that there was no competing drawing there — but **Plain Ground — the Glass Is Retired** gives decoration on a page nothing either, so the component was deleted rather than scoped, and `DataFlower` survives only as a mark standing *on* a page. What is left behind the organism is nothing at all: the single faint grid this rule once permitted as "paper rather than tissue" came out too, under **The Ground Is Black, and Nothing Is Drawn On It**, which is the end state of the same argument. The corona is filament work: it stays hidden until the view is pulled back past a fifth and reaches full strength by two-thirds, by which point the organism itself has shrunk out of legibility. All backdrop tuning lives in the `BACKDROP` constant and the `Backdrop` component in `Orrery.tsx` — never scatter background opacities through the render.

## GR-04 — Legibility Comes From the Ground, Not From Thicker Strands

When the tissue is hard to read, empty the background before touching the drawing. Filaments stay hairlines (~0.25–0.45 before weight); contrast is bought with opacity — resting edge bundles at ~0.8 (spine ~0.95), the halo bed at 0.17 of that, soma membranes at ~0.1 fill. Thickening a strand turns a bundle back into a stroke, which is the one thing the grammar exists to avoid.

## GR-05 — Black, White, and One Temperature of Light

Ink is monochrome without exception — every stroke, filament, soma, bead and letterform is `currentColor`, and translucent whites/blacks (opacity steps) still provide all depth. Hue exists **only in emitted light**: the `--lume-*` variables reach the innermost two stops of the `-core` radial gradient and the swell under `.membrane-lit`, and nothing else. Light has a colour because light always does; line does not, because line is structure. If a hue ever lands on a stroke, the rule has been broken. Two registers — `ember` and `cyanotype` — alternate by chapter so the dossier is *felt* to change temperature rather than seen to change palette, and they are warm and cool **whites**, not amber and cyan. The variables live on `:root` and are stamped on `<html>`, never on the app shell: the figure stage is portalled to `<body>`, so anything scoped to the shell is invisible on the one surface where figures are largest. **Verify by measurement, not by eye** — rasterise a surface that paints `url(#…-core)` twice, once with the live stops and once forced to white, and require: mean saturation < 22%, max saturation ≤ 22%, and no pixel whose luma moves more than 12/255 between the two. Measured on the map at the current values: mean 2.46%, max 15.79%, zero pixels shifted. An earlier pair of halo stops at 27% chroma failed this and was softened, not argued for.

## GR-06 — A Cell That Emits No Light Takes No Temperature

The chapter opener draws no glow behind its soma — deliberately, and twice-tested: a radial disc read as a bulb, and a membrane-shaped blur read as a grey cloud, because any soft mass behind fine white filaments lifts the black to grey and takes the drawing's contrast with it. It therefore shows no light temperature either, and that is correct rather than a gap. Do not add a glow to the opener to make the register visible there; the register is a property of light, and the opener's argument is that density of line is enough.

## GR-07 — Blur a Line, Never a Mass

The law behind every soft pass in the app. **A Figure Has a Far Plane** owns the mechanism and the measured settings; this rule only supplies the reason one is permitted to exist at all, because the same blur has both worked and failed here. A membrane-shaped blur was tried and removed for reading as a grey cloud (**A Cell That Emits No Light Takes No Temperature**), while the far plane's Gaussian is load-bearing — and the difference between them is not the radius, it is what lay underneath. **Blur a mass and you get fog; blur a line and you still have a line, with black beside it.** A blurred filament keeps the gaps between its strokes; a blurred fill has none to keep. So a soft pass may fall only on line geometry, sparse enough that the ground survives between the strokes, and never on a fill, a gradient or a silhouette. Wherever one is added the gate is measured, not judged (**Verify by measurement, not by eye**): rasterise the surface with the pass and without it, and require the ground not to lift — mean luma of non-drawing pixels within 3/255, with the sharp tissue's contrast unchanged. If the black lifts it is fog, and it comes out however it looked.

## GR-08 — The Reader Is Made of Light-Wells, Not Panels

Nothing in the book section is a box. A surface is a `.membrane` (`.membrane-faint` nested, `.membrane-lit` active) — which under **Plain Ground** draws nothing at all unless it sits over other content, in which case it is solid theme ground. A control is a `.bud` — a swell of light under the glyph that grows on hover; never a bordered pill and never a black↔white fill flip. Chrome bands are `.veil` (`.veil-up` / `.veil-left` / `.veil-right` for direction), overlays are `.scrim`, the search modal is `.well`. Dividers are `<Vein />` and markers are `<Soma />` / `<SomaLabel />` from `src/components/organic/Organic.tsx` — a curved filament that fades to nothing at both ends and a glow with two dendrite hairs, never a rule and never a square bullet. Flat translucent fills are exactly what read as grey slabs, which is most of why the glass came out. A `.membrane` must never be the scroll container itself — an absolutely positioned field inside a scroller scrolls away from its content; put the well on the frame and scroll the column inside it.
