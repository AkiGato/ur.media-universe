Teaching programme

Attention, ethics and narrative

Three four-week studio courses on what communication design does to the people who receive it. Any one stands alone. Taken together they are a sequence.

Three coursesFour weeks each48 contact hours Studio, in EnglishNo prerequisites

All three are about one thing: a human nervous system receiving something that was built to affect it. What separates them is where the student is standing when they look at it.

Diagnosis

What is being done, and why the model that does it breaks.

Obligation

What a designer owes the person on the other side.

Construction

What else these mechanics could be for.

Course one · the attention economy

Media as Universe

Why were we never built for this — and what breaks first in a business that depends on us not noticing?
Week 1
The mechanics

Name every weapon operating on you, and find the four places the model that uses them is structurally weak.

Week 2
The person

Stop writing to profiles. Write to the state someone is actually in when the message arrives.

Week 3
The claim

Take a commercial claim apart, down to its economic, historical and cognitive roots.

Week 4
The proof

Build one thing. Measure what it costs the people who receive it. Read the number out loud.

Students leave with

  • One finished artifact
  • A measured result, obtained under their own protocol
  • A sourced causal analysis of a real claim

Suits

Advertising and communication, marketing and brand strategy, design, media studies, business. The strongest fit of the three for a communication faculty, and the easiest to justify commercially.

Course two · the designer's obligation

Human-Centric and Neuroethical Design

If every interface decision activates or suppresses a cognitive system, what is a designer responsible for?
Week 1
The act

Design as a neurological decision, made daily, at scale, on an unconsenting population.

Week 2
The person

What behavioural data cannot know. Vulnerability as a state anyone enters, not a category of person.

Week 3
The line

Consent where there is no consent form, and what is now actionable rather than merely objectionable.

Week 4
The review

Defend your verdict to a room arguing in good faith for the product you condemned.

Students leave with

  • A design review document on a real product
  • A verdict they can hold, or amend honestly
  • An account of what is actionable, not just wrong

Suits

Design and HCI, product and engineering, psychology, media law and policy. The most portable of the three — it needs a room and nothing else, and runs unchanged as professional training.

Course three · narrative and play

Alternative Storytelling

Why is a story retained when a fact is not — and when is a loop a gift rather than a trap?
Week 1
The pact

Disbelief suspended by consent. A fiction you have agreed to is not a lie — and what generative media did to that.

Week 2
The tests

Who holds the goal. Are the mechanics disclosable. Does leaving cost nothing. Then find the one edit at which a mechanic flips.

Week 3
The turn

The same mechanics made extractive, the biases they run on, and where the clinician's boundary sits.

Week 4
The proof

Build it, measure it, then let the players say in their own words what they thought they agreed to.

Students leave with

  • One playable or readable artifact
  • A mechanic-by-mechanic justification, rejections included
  • A measured result, and their players' account of it

Suits

Education and instructional design, serious games and interaction design, health communication, museum and cultural programming. Taught through three built projects: an XR escape room, a flow game, and a journal game on paper.

The obvious problem

One course forbids streaks. Another teaches you to build with them.

Both positions are held deliberately. What reconciles them is a definition, not a compromise.

The mechanic is not the unit of judgement. The consent is.

They built worlds — storytelling, gamification, branded fictions — and invited the audience inside. Trust was not required, because the audience had entered the pact that cinema and literature rely on: disbelief suspended by consent. A fiction you have agreed to is not a lie. Media as Universe, §2.1

A streak serving a habit someone chose supports that person's own intention. The identical streak serving a subscription they are trying to cancel extracts from them. Same code, opposite direction of agency. Three things keep this from being a licence: the person must hold the goal, the mechanics must survive being disclosed, and the exit must cost nothing.

The third has a provenance worth stating, because it is what separates this from a position arrived at for convenience. It was not reasoned out. It was noticed by a designer about her own game, built deliberately for flow, with every one of flow's conditions satisfied:

The ethical question was, this flow was capturing, this boat could become a safe place one couldn't easily leave. On Cloudy Horizons, 2025

A practitioner catching her own successful design in the act, and treating it as a problem rather than a retention metric, is the whole programme in one sentence. The three courses are the method for arriving there reliably, from whichever side you approach it.

What holds it together

Four shared instruments

Students use them on their own output in week four, not on a case study.
InstrumentWhat it does
The five-question gateFive questions run before anything ships, two of them hard gates. A mechanic that cannot be disclosed does not pass, and softening the language is not a fix.
The restoration readingThree questions before an interaction and three after, averaged. Measures the state the person is left in, indifferent to which mechanics produced it — the same wording and thresholds in all three courses, so results compare.
Three cognitive posturesDesigned analytical categories describing what a person can receive at the moment of contact, used instead of demographic or psychographic segments.
The cost of leavingWhether a person can stop without it costing them, counted in steps, in lost progress, and in how the design makes going feel.
