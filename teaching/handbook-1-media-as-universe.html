01

The terms

Read these first. They are unusual enough that students who assume the normal rules lose marks in the first week for the wrong reasons.

On volume

One artifact, not a portfolio.

You will build one thing and build it properly. Nothing is gained by submitting more. The course argues that every piece of content is a claim on a fixed resource; producing a portfolio to look industrious would contradict the thing you are being taught.

On measurement

A poor result, honestly obtained, scores above a good one with no method.

If your artifact depletes the people who receive it and you can say why, you are doing the work. If it restores them and you cannot show how you measured, you are not. The report is assessed on protocol and interpretation, never on the number.

On attendance

No marks for participation, attendance or effort.

There is no register, no points, no visible progress and nothing to keep up. A course arguing that engagement mechanics are a design failure cannot be run on them, and you should say so if you catch it happening.

On the framework

You are invited to break it.

One standing assignment runs the whole four weeks: find the case where this framework gives the wrong answer. A counter-example you can defend is worth more to this course than agreement, and it is recorded rather than argued away.

02

Four weeks

What each week asks, and what you will be holding at the end of it.

Week one

The mechanics and the market

How the attention economy extracts, what it selects for, and where the model is structurally weak. You stop describing mechanics and start naming them.

You will holdA named mechanics inventory for a real product, and a one-page structural verdict on a real brand.
Week two

Reading the person

Why the profile fails on an individual, what directed attention fatigue does to a decision, and how to write to a state rather than a segment.

You will holdOne brief written three times, once per posture, with one version discarded at the gate and the discard defended in writing.
Week three

Reading the claim

Taking a commercial claim apart: who benefits from this framing continuing, what it extends, and what it activates. Your build brief is issued at the end of the week.

You will holdA sourced root-node analysis, a sentence naming what the claim did not disclose, and a signed constraint sheet.
Week four

Build, measure, defend

You build one artifact under the constraints you set yourself, measure what it does to real people, and read the result out before you explain it.

You will holdThe artifact, a delta study with its protocol, and a defence given out loud to the room.

Constraint sheet

Paper three · session 3.4 · cannot be revised

You sign this before you build, and it does not change afterwards. Name three mechanics you are forbidding yourself and what you will do instead of each. The point is not that the list is long but that it is binding: in session 4.4 you present the artifact against this sheet, and a mechanic that appears in the work after being forbidden here costs more than never having forbidden it.

Choose things you would genuinely have reached for. A constraint sheet that forbids mechanics you had no use for is a sheet that forbids nothing.

Root-node worksheet

Delta recording form

Paper five · session 4.3

One row per subject. Three readings before the interaction, three immediately after, each from one to five. The delta is the after-reading minus the before, averaged across the three. At or above +0.5 is restorative; between −0.5 and +0.5, neutral; at or below −0.5, depleting.

Subjects are numbered, never named. Write the numbers down before anyone in your team discusses them — a reading recorded after the conversation about what you hoped for is not a reading.

The three questions, asked exactly as written: Right now, how easily could you concentrate on something that requires effort? (1 not at all, 5 easily) · Right now, how much does anything feel urgent or demanding of you? (1 everything, 5 nothing) · Right now, could you make a considered decision, or only react? 04

Without looking it up

These are the things you will be asked to use in the room, at speed, without a screen. You are not asked to recite them. You are asked to apply them, which is harder and is what the papers above are for.

Three cognitive postures

PostureThe stateWhat it must not be offered
Restoration-seekingSystem 1 running everything, System 2 gone. Present, but nothing lands with weight. Reaching for relief, not information.Anything with urgency in it. The most exploited state in the history of marketing and the most indefensible to target.
Agency-seekingSystem 2 engaged, directed attention recovered enough for deliberate action. Arrived with an intention.Manufactured urgency deployed before the assessment can finish — timers, stock counters, social proof at the moment of consideration.
Meaning-seekingTesting whether what you stand for is consistent with who they are trying to become.Anything inauthentic. This audience is the most sophisticated detector of inauthenticity in existence, and it is expanding.

Four fractures of the Fragility Index

  1. The monopoly problem. Distribution conducted on someone else's property, where the landlord writes the lease and changes it mid-tenancy.
  2. Attention is finite. In an information-rich world the scarce resource is not content but the attention required to receive it.
  3. Regulatory lag is closing. The operating model was calibrated for an environment that no longer exists, and cannot be retrofitted at the pace the compliance wave moves.
  4. Borrowed trust. Platform credibility, influencer authority and algorithmic reach are rented from systems whose trust is in measurable decline.

Three causal roots, and the question each asks

  • Economic — who benefits from this situation continuing, and who loses if it changes?
  • Historical — what precedent does this extend, and what decision created the conditions it is living inside?
  • Neuro-psychological — what human cognitive pattern is being activated, exploited or expressed?

Four properties of a restorative environment

  • Being away — a signal, immediate and effortless, that nothing urgent is required here.
  • Extent — a sense that there is more to explore than the immediate field of vision presents.
  • Fascination — effortless, involuntary engagement. Holds attention without demanding it; suspends the stress response rather than activating it.
  • Compatibility — the environment supports what the person came to do. The component extractive design violates most aggressively.

Five anti-engagement metrics

  1. Cognitive clarity score — can the person explain the causal structure of what they received?
  2. Agency index — did they take a deliberate action, or a triggered one? Measured through time-to-decision.
  3. Restoration delta — did this deplete directed attention or replenish it? The one you will run yourself.
  4. Trust compounding rate — does trust increase over repeated interactions? Distinguishes satisfaction from trust.
  5. Meaning alignment score — did the communication reinforce or undermine coherence between values and choices?

Eight branches of the production ruleset

BranchReach for it when

Above all eight sits one test, which is never overridden: would this piece still work if the recipient could see exactly how and why it was built to affect them? If not, redesign the approach rather than softening the language.

If the mechanic is one you think helped you

You will hit this in week one. The deck names streaks, badges and variable reward as weapons, and your language app uses all three, and you believe it worked. That is a fair objection and it has an answer: the mechanic is not the unit of judgement — the consent is. A streak serving a habit you chose supports your own intention; the identical streak serving a subscription you are trying to cancel extracts from you. Same code, opposite direction of agency.

Three questions settle any particular case. Does the person hold the goal, or did the system supply it? Would the mechanic still work if they could see exactly how it was built to affect them? Does leaving cost them nothing? This course is about the case where all three fail, which is most commercial deployment of them. Raise the counter-example anyway — it is the standing assignment, and the third course of this programme is where the other case gets built.

06

Consent

You will ask real people for an account of their own cognitive state. You write the protocol before you collect anything, and it must contain all seven of these.

  1. Subjects volunteer, and are told what the readings are for before they give any.
  2. A subject may stop at any point, including after giving the before-readings, and nothing is inferred from a withdrawal.
  3. No names and no student identifiers. Subjects are numbered.
  4. No demographic fields beyond what your analysis actually requires. In almost every case that is none.
  5. Readings go on paper, or into a form your team controls. Not into a third-party tool.
  6. Only aggregate figures leave the room.
  7. You may withhold your own readings from the cohort register without any effect on your grade, and you do not have to give a reason.

The papers on this page hold nothing and send nothing — drafts stay in your own browser. That is deliberate and it is the same standard you are being asked to meet: an instrument built to teach the cost of silent collection cannot collect silently.

07

Reading

Four of these do the structural work and the rest are referred to as needed. Read the first two before week one if you read nothing else.

  • Kahneman, D. (2011). Thinking, Fast and Slow. — The two systems, loss aversion, the availability heuristic, and why a statistical profile collapses on a named individual.
  • Williams, J. (2018). Stand Out of Our Light. — The attentional environment as structural rather than conspiratorial; impulsive against reflective will; agency as the measure of a technology.
  • Taleb, N.N. (2012). Antifragile. — Fragility as dependence on a counterparty's stability, and why systems optimised for short-term efficiency go blind to their own fragility.
  • Kaplan, S. & Kaplan, R. (1989). The Experience of Nature. — Attention restoration theory and the four properties. The source of the design criteria and of the delta instrument.
  • Cialdini, R.B. (1984, 2016). Influence; Pre-Suasion. — The principles, and the engineering of the moment before the message.
  • Sunstein, C.R. (2016). Fifty Shades of Manipulation; The Ethics of Influence. — The criterion behind questions three and four.
  • Simon, H.A. (1971). Designing Organizations for an Information-Rich World. — Attention as the scarce resource. Short, and the origin of the content budget.
  • Eagleman, D. (2011, 2015); Doidge, N. (2007). — The unconscious architecture, and the brain restructuring itself around repeated use.
