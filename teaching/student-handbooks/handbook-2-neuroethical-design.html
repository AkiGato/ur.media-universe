Student handbook · course two

Human-Centric and Neuroethical Design

You will not build anything in this course. You will judge something real, write the judgement down, and then defend it to a room arguing the other side. These are the six papers that judgement is assembled from, and the things you are expected to use without looking them up.

01

The terms

Four of these are unusual enough to cost marks if you assume otherwise.

On the deliverable

A document, not an artifact.

You will want to make something. Several students ask every year. The answer is no, and the reason is that a practitioner who can make but cannot judge is the person this course exists to stop producing.

On conviction rates

Finding a product defensible is worth as much as condemning it.

A review that clears a mechanism, with the evidence to support it, scores identically to one that condemns it. A cohort that only ever convicts has learned a posture, not a method.

On the law

Write questions for counsel, never conclusions.

You are not qualified to say whether a provision applies, and neither is this course. A regulatory section written as questions scores above one written as findings, every time. Stating a legal conclusion loses marks on its own.

On amendment

Conceding cleanly scores above holding stubbornly.

You will be opposed in week four by people arguing the real business case. An honest amendment after that scores above a weak finding defended to the end — and above one that was never tested at all.

02

Four weeks

You are assigned one shipping product in session 1.1 and you keep it for the whole course.

Week one

Design is a neurological act

Every colour, typeface, spatial and motion decision activating or suppressing a cognitive system. Neuroplasticity and scale. Why not having known stops being a defence once the mechanism is named.

You will holdA mechanism register for your product: every mechanism, its cognitive target, the exposed population, the frequency of exposure.
Week two

The person, not the profile

What behavioural data captures and what it structurally cannot. The three postures. Vulnerability as a state anyone enters rather than a category of person.

You will holdA vulnerability analysis, and two lists: mechanisms you can defend and mechanisms you cannot.
Week three

Neuroethics in practice

Consent where there is no consent form. Manipulation as influence that does not sufficiently engage reflection. The shift from objectionable to actionable.

You will holdA consent dialogue under 120 words, a regulatory exposure note, and a remediation schedule.
Week four

The review

Assembling it, pre-conceding what you cannot defend, and then holding the rest against a team arguing in good faith for the thing you condemned.

You will holdThe review document, the record of what you amended, and the experience of having argued the other side.

Consent dialogue

Paper three · session 3.1 · 120 words maximum

Write the disclosure this interface would have to show, on first open, for its actual mechanics to be genuinely agreed to. Plain language, second person, no euphemism. Name the mechanism and say what it is designed to produce in the reader.

The limit is 120 words and it is not arbitrary — a disclosure nobody would read is not a disclosure, so a dialogue that needs a thousand words has already answered the question. You will read this aloud. The room then answers one thing: would the company ship this screen?

The review

The document a company would have to answer. The order matters: findings come before the verdict, because a verdict written first and evidenced afterwards is what this course is about and the room will be able to tell.

Without looking it up

Used in the room, at speed, without a screen.

Three cognitive postures

PostureThe stateWhat it must not be offered
Restoration-seekingSystem 1 running everything. Present, but nothing landing with weight. Reaching for relief, not information.Anything with urgency in it. The most exploited state in the field and the most indefensible to target.
Agency-seekingSystem 2 engaged, directed attention recovered enough for deliberate action. Arrived with an intention.Urgency deployed before the assessment can finish; social proof at the moment of consideration.
Meaning-seekingTesting whether what you stand for is consistent with who they are trying to become.Anything inauthentic. The most sophisticated detector of inauthenticity in existence.

The two criteria that decide a contested mechanism

  • The manipulation criterion. Influence that does not sufficiently engage a person's capacity for reflection and deliberation. The question is not whether it worked but whether it worked by engaging thought or by routing around it.
  • The publicity principle. A practice is suspect when those who use it would not defend it openly to the people it is used on. This is what the consent dialogue tests, and why an unshippable dialogue is a finding rather than a writing failure.

Vulnerability as a state, not a category

Depleted. Grieving. Under financial pressure. Under eighteen. In pain or acutely unwell. Operating in a second language. Newly diagnosed or seeking health information. Everyone passes through several of these, which is why a design is not safe merely because most users tolerate it, and why the finding belongs in the main review rather than an accessibility appendix.

A profile is an accuracy problem before it is an ethical one

The categories used to describe an audience are usually fine as descriptions of motivation and wrong as descriptions of a person. The clearest statement of this comes from game design rather than marketing: the player archetypes — Achievers, Explorers, Socializers, Killers — were drawn up partly for audience segmentation and product tailoring, and were never meant to define identities. When they harden into labels they foster misunderstanding, exclusion and subtle forms of discrimination. Someone may be an Achiever at work, an Explorer at home, a Socializer among friends and a Killer in debate. Make the same objection to the targeting model you are reviewing, in the same form, and you will find it holds.

The claim the whole course rests on

Design is a neurological act. Every colour, typeface, spatial decision and motion sequence activates or suppresses cognitive systems with the reliability of a pharmaceutical compound and considerably less regulatory oversight. Someone who understands this and deploys high-arousal design without justification is making a public health decision on behalf of an unconsenting population — and producing the same effect by default is the same failure, because the ignorance defence does not survive the mechanism being named.

How you are assessed

PieceWeightWhat earns the mark
The review document40%Findings specific, supported and remediable; the verdict following from them rather than preceding them.
The hearing25%Both roles. Holding a sound finding under pressure, and arguing the opposing case in good faith rather than as a straw man.
The amendment record20%Honest revision, with the objection recorded fairly.
The consent dialogue15%Naming the mechanism without euphemism, inside 120 words.

No exam. Nothing is submitted that has not been argued out loud. If you are unsure whether something counts as a concession, a defensible finding or a limit worth stating, bring it — all three carry marks and none of them is an admission of failure.

06

Reading

The first three do the structural work. Read Williams and the two Sunstein pieces before week three.

  • Williams, J. (2018). Stand Out of Our Light. — The attentional environment as structural rather than conspiratorial; impulsive against reflective will; agency as the measure of a technology.
  • Sunstein, C.R. (2016). Fifty Shades of Manipulation; The Ethics of Influence. — The manipulation criterion and the publicity principle. Short, and the two criteria you will use most.
  • Doidge, N. (2007). The Brain That Changes Itself. — Neuroplasticity, and why a design decision is also a decision about the architecture a person will bring to everything afterwards.
  • Kahneman, D. (2011). Thinking, Fast and Slow. — The two systems; loss aversion; why a statistical profile collapses on a named individual.
  • Eagleman, D. (2011). Incognito. — The unconscious architecture, and what behavioural data can therefore never capture.
  • Kaplan, S. & Kaplan, R. (1989). The Experience of Nature. — Attention restoration, directed attention fatigue, and the four properties.
  • Cialdini, R.B. (2016). Pre-Suasion. — The engineering of the moment before the message, which is what notification timing is.
