Facilitator's kit

Antifragile Communication Design

A four-week studio course on attention, persuasion mechanics and the cost of what we build. Students name the mechanics operating on them, design one artifact against a stated standard, measure what it does to the people who receive it, and report the number whether or not it flatters them.

4 weeks · 16 sessions 48 contact hours Studio format
01

The map

Four weeks, four sessions each, three hours a session. Each week ends with something made, not something covered. The seminar beat is never longer than forty minutes, because a course about directed attention cannot be delivered as a two-hour slide performance.

Week one

The mechanics and the market

How the attention economy extracts. Dual-process cognition under high-arousal design. The four fractures of the Fragility Index — monopoly, finite attention, closing regulatory lag, borrowed trust.

OutputA named mechanics inventory, and a structural fragility verdict on a real brand.
Week two

Reading the person

Why statistical profiles collapse on individuals. Directed attention fatigue. Pre-suasion. The three cognitive postures as an operational replacement for psychographic segments.

OutputOne campaign rewritten three times, one version discarded at the gate.
Week three

Reading the claim

Surface against structure. The Causal Taxonomy: decomposing a commercial claim into its economic, historical and neuro-psychological roots. Build phase opens.

OutputA root-node analysis, and the thing the claim did not disclose.
Week four

Build, measure, defend

The four properties of restorative environments as design criteria. The anti-engagement metrics. One artifact, built under the production ruleset, measured on live subjects.

OutputThe artifact, its Restoration Delta, and a public defence.
02

Run sheets

One sheet per session, written to be read standing up. The question is what the session answers. The move is what the room does for the remaining two hours.

Week one — The mechanics and the market

1.1

Why were we never built for this?

Beat
The scale mismatch. A nervous system calibrated for a village receiving a planet. System 1 and System 2, and what happens when one is knocked off the rails.
Move
Each student writes down, from memory, the last three things they bought or watched without deciding to. Read aloud, no commentary, no analysis yet. The list is the course's first dataset.
Output
A wall of unchosen actions.
1.2

What are the actual weapons?

Beat
Cialdini's principles inverted. Scarcity as manufactured cortisol. Social proof as a tether. Variable reward, where the unpredictability is the mechanism and the content does not need to be good.
Move
The Weapons Deck, two rounds.
Output
A shared vocabulary. From here on a mechanic gets named, not described.
1.3

What does the engine actually select for?

Beat
The high-arousal filter. The viral quadrant. Why the system cannot monetise calm, and why the blind spot — a reaction is a reaction — makes outrage the optimal output rather than a failure.
Move
Teams audit one assigned product and produce the mechanics inventory: every scarcity cue, social-proof counter and variable-reward surface, each with a screenshot and the principle it inverts.
Output
The mechanics inventory. This is assessed.
1.4

Where is this model structurally weak?

Beat
Fragility as dependence on a counterparty's stability. The four fractures. The landlord, not the infrastructure.
Move
Teach the Meta boycott and the Patagonia contrast as the worked example, then teams score a brand of their own choosing against all four fractures.
Output
A one-page structural verdict, written in the register of the case studies.

Week two — Reading the person

2.1

Why does the profile not work?

Beat
Demographics to psychographics to predictive modelling, each transition felt like precision and was a deeper dehumanisation. Why statistical probability collapses into noise on a named individual. Behavioural data as the shadow a person left behind.
Move
Hand each team a real audience segment from a real media kit. They write the person it claims to describe, then write the three people it would describe equally well who would need opposite communication.
Output
Three counter-examples per segment.
2.2

What state is the person in?

Beat
Directed attention as a finite resource that depletes with use. What happens at the ceiling: System 2 steps down, System 1 takes over. Soft fascination as the opposite condition. Pre-suasion, and the feed as pre-suasive architecture.
Move
Run the Restoration Delta on the room, cold, before anyone has been taught the instrument. Record the readings. Then teach what was just measured.
Output
A baseline reading for the cohort, kept for week four.
2.3

Can you write for a state instead of a profile?

Beat
The three postures — restoration-seeking, agency-seeking, meaning-seeking. Designed analytical categories, not discovered psychological types and not personas. What each posture can actually receive.
Move
Posture Roulette, three rounds, then the same brief written properly for all three postures.
Output
Three posture-specific treatments of one brief.
2.4

Would it survive being explained?

Beat
The five questions as an operational standard rather than a checklist. Questions three and four restate Sunstein's criterion for manipulation and the publicity principle behind it; what the dossier adds is the operationalisation, at production scale.
Move
Build the Worst Thing. Then every team runs its own three treatments through the gate and discards whichever fails.
Output
Two surviving treatments and a written defence of the discard. The discard is assessed.

Week three — Reading the claim

3.1

What has the headline already done?

Beat
The availability heuristic: vivid information surfaces faster, feels more true, and carries disproportionate weight afterwards. Confirmation bias compounding it. The outrage headline as a product that installs a lens, not a journalistic failure.
Move
Students bring a commercial claim that made them feel something before they had finished reading it. The room identifies what was installed.
Output
A set of live claims to work on for the rest of the week.
3.2

Who benefits from this framing continuing?

Beat
Surface against structure. The economic root: every narrative has a budget, and some framings are more promotable than others without anyone being corrupt.
Move
The Root Hunt, economic register only. Evidence required, cleverness not scored.
Output
The economic branch of each team's root-node diagram.
3.3

What does this extend, and what does it activate?

Beat
The historical root: context as the load-bearing architecture of the present, and the facts being accurate while the framing still lies. The neuro-psychological root: loss aversion, belonging anxiety, status competition. Triangulated reading.
Move
The Root Hunt completed across both remaining registers, then each diagram presented in four minutes.
Output
The full root-node diagram, and one sentence naming what the claim did not disclose. This is assessed.
3.4

What are you going to build?

Beat
The four properties of restorative environments as design criteria: being away, extent, fascination, compatibility. Compatibility is the one extractive design violates most aggressively.
Move
Briefs issued. Each team takes one branch of the production ruleset and writes down, before building, the three mechanics it is forbidding itself and what it will do instead.
Output
A signed constraint sheet. It cannot be revised after this session.

Week four — Build, measure, defend

4.1

What gets measured instead?

Beat
What gets measured gets built. The five anti-engagement metrics: cognitive clarity, agency index, restoration delta, trust compounding rate, meaning alignment. Not replacements for commercial measurement — the layer beneath it.
Move
Build, supervised. Facilitator circulates and asks one question only: which mechanic did you just reach for, and why did you stop.
Output
Working drafts.
4.2

How much of a person's week is it fair to claim?

Beat
Content minimisation. A budget, not a target: a campaign spends a budget and then owes a silence. A plan that decays without constant presence is a dependency, not a strategy.
Move
Build continues. Each team must cut one piece from its own plan and say what the cut buys.
Output
The artifact, finished, plus a stated budget.
4.3

What did it cost the person who received it?

Beat
The Restoration Delta: three questions before, three after, averaged, banded at plus or minus a half point. It is self-report, and self-report is what the standard asks for.
Move
The Delta Duel, run on volunteer subjects from another studio under the consent protocol.
Output
Paired readings, written down before anyone discusses them.
4.4

Would you be comfortable if they could see all of it?

Beat
None. The facilitator does not speak for the first hour.
Move
The Fifth Question. Each team presents the artifact, the delta, the constraint sheet it signed in 3.4, and an answer to question five. The room may ask, but not congratulate.
Output
The defence. Assessed.
03

Six moves

Five of these are games and the sixth deliberately is not. None of them awards points that persist, keeps a leaderboard, or records a streak. A course arguing that engagement mechanics are a design failure cannot run on them, and students notice the inconsistency faster than faculty do.

Session 1.2 · 25 minutes · teams of three

The Weapons Deck

A card describes a mechanic as a person actually meets it. Teams have sixty seconds to name three things: the principle it inverts, the cognitive system it targets, and where the production ruleset forbids it.

  1. Draw a card. Read it aloud. No screen, no search.
  2. Teams write their three answers face down.
  3. Reveal. A team scores only on all three being right.
  4. Any team may challenge the answer on the card. A successful challenge is worth more than a correct answer, and the card gets rewritten.

The challenge rule is the point. The deck is a draft, not an authority, and the mechanics students bring from their own market are usually the ones missing from it. The most common challenge is also the most legitimate: a student will argue that the streak or the badge on the card is the same mechanic their own learning app uses to help them. They are right, and the answer is the one in the teaching notes below.

Session 2.4 · 70 minutes · teams of four

Build the Worst Thing

Each team gets a legitimate product and twenty minutes to design the most extractive campaign for it they can. Every mechanic permitted. Nothing held back.

  1. Twenty minutes to build it. Encourage the room to compete on ruthlessness.
  2. Then the condition is revealed: you will present it alongside a full, honest narration of every mechanism you used and the state it was designed to exploit.
  3. Present both together, out loud, to the room.
  4. The room votes on one question: would this still work?

This is the most important exercise in the course. Question three — would these mechanics still work if the person could see them clearly — is an argument when it is asserted and an experience when the room watches a campaign collapse under its own narration. Nobody forgets it, and no later session needs to re-argue it.

Session 2.3 · 30 minutes · individual

Posture Roulette

Draw a posture, a product and a moment. Write the opening line, and write the one thing you will refuse to do.

  1. Draw three. The combination is often awkward; that is the exercise.
  2. Four minutes to write the opening line and the refusal.
  3. Read the line only. Withhold the posture.
  4. The room guesses which posture it was written for.

If the room cannot tell, the line was written for nobody. This converts the postures from three paragraphs students can recite into a constraint they can feel the absence of.

Sessions 3.2–3.3 · across two sessions · teams of four

The Root Hunt

Every team works the same commercial claim and digs for the deepest defensible root in each of the three registers.

  1. Each root must arrive with a source the room can check.
  2. A root with no source scores nothing, however good it sounds.
  3. Depth beats quantity: one root traced three steps back outranks five roots named once.
  4. Any team may retire a root it can no longer defend. Retiring costs nothing. Defending something indefensible costs the round.

Scoring evidence rather than ingenuity is what separates the taxonomy from a conspiracy framework, and the distinction has to be enforced by the rules, not stated in the lecture.

Session 4.3 · 90 minutes · pairs of teams

The Delta Duel

Two teams build two versions of the same thing. Both get measured on the same volunteer subjects, and both numbers get read out.

  1. One version is built under the ruleset. The other is built to convert, with the mechanics permitted.
  2. Volunteers from another studio, briefed and consenting, give three readings before and three after.
  3. Record the delta and, separately, whatever conversion proxy the brief allows.
  4. Read both numbers. Do not reconcile them.

The extractive version frequently wins on conversion and loses on delta. That split is the dossier's own worked example, and watching it happen with their own numbers is the moment students stop treating the ethical argument as a preference.

Session 4.4 · 90 minutes · whole cohort

The Fifth Question

Not a game. No teams, no scoring, no winner. Each student reads their artifact's delta aloud, then answers question five in their own words.

  1. The number is read before the explanation, not after.
  2. A bad number is read in the same tone as a good one.
  3. The room may ask questions. The room may not congratulate.
  4. The facilitator says nothing until everyone has finished.

The course spends three weeks arguing that measurement is only worth something if the measurer reports it honestly when it goes against them. This session is where that gets practised rather than asserted, which is why it is deliberately the one session with nothing enjoyable about it.

05

The loop back

The course is also the research instrument. Each cohort produces five things the dossier currently does not have, and every one of them improves the framework rather than merely transmitting it.

One — the delta register

The dossier states plainly that it holds no research figure for what a restorative interaction is worth, and that a calculator producing one without showing its arithmetic would fail its own third and fourth questions. A cohort of twenty students, two artifacts each, eight subjects apiece, produces roughly three hundred paired readings. Three cohorts is a dataset. A dataset is the paper, and the paper is the thing that moves this from a well-argued master's thesis to a citable instrument.

Two — the bait taxonomy grows

Every mechanics inventory names mechanics the dossier did not. This matters most where the thesis is weakest: it is built on Meta, Patagonia and a largely Western case base, and the mechanics students bring from their own markets are exactly what is missing. The challenge rule in the Weapons Deck exists to capture them.

Three — the ruleset gets stress-tested

Twenty students taking eight branches into real briefs will find the branches that cannot actually be worked with. Those findings are amendments, not complaints. Run the course twice and the production ruleset is no longer eight blocks of prose written by one person — it is eight blocks that survived contact with sixty people trying to use them under deadline.

Four — the trade-off curve

The Delta Duel produces paired conversion and delta figures on the same subjects. The dossier asserts the trade-off and does not quantify it. Quantifying it is the single most useful thing this course could contribute to practice, because it replaces an ethical appeal with a price: this is what that conversion rate cost, in the recipient's own account of their state.

Five — the counter-examples

Set one standing assignment: break the framework. Find the case where a restorative design loses on every measure, or where the taxonomy produces a reading that is clearly wrong. Record what the students find in the open-questions document rather than defending the framework against it. A framework with a published list of the places it fails is harder to dismiss than one without.

The consent protocol

Readings are self-report from volunteers, so the protocol is not optional and students should be made to write it out before the duel, not handed it. Subjects volunteer and may stop at any point. No names, no student identifiers, no demographic fields beyond what the analysis actually requires. Readings go on paper or into a form the instructor controls. Aggregate figures only leave the room. Any student may withhold their own readings from the register without affecting their grade, and the facilitator says this out loud in session 4.3 rather than putting it in a document nobody reads.

06

Assessment

No exam. Four pieces, all produced in the room, all defended out loud.

PieceWeightGraded on
The artifact 40% Whether it holds the constraint sheet signed in session 3.4, and whether the four restorative properties are visible in the thing rather than claimed about it.
The delta study and report 25% Method and honesty, not score. A student who measures −1.3 and explains the mechanism earns more than one who reports +2.0 without a protocol.
The five-question defence 20% The quality of the discard. What was rejected and why carries the mark, not what survived.
The causal decomposition 15% Whether each root is sourced and traceable, and whether the undisclosed element is correctly identified.

Two grading rules worth stating to the cohort in session 1.1, because they change how students work from the first week. The first: a poor measured result, honestly obtained and correctly interpreted, scores above a good one with no method behind it. The second: there are no marks for participation, attendance or volume of work, and one artifact is the deliverable rather than a portfolio of them. Content minimisation applies to coursework or it does not apply to anything.

07

Teaching notes

Ways this course can quietly contradict itself, and what to do instead.

End on time, every time. An overrunning seminar is a claim on directed attention made by someone who has just finished explaining why such claims need justifying. Stopping mid-sentence at the hour is a better lesson than finishing the thought.

Do not gamify attendance. No participation points, no streaks, no public progress. The games have winners announced in the room and recorded nowhere, which is the distinction the course is about.

Teach from the drawings, not from slides. The diagrams, the postures, the fragility index and the root-node figure already exist as working figures. A deck rebuilt in a presentation tool will drift from them and will be denser than the original. Open the figure itself.

Let the room challenge the framework. The Weapons Deck has a challenge rule, the Root Hunt has a retirement rule and the standing assignment is to break the thing. A course that treats a master's thesis as settled teaches students to defer, which is the opposite of what the five questions ask them to do.

On the fourth chapter in a sensitive setting. The Causal Taxonomy works identically on a commercial claim, a category narrative or a product launch as it does on a news event, and the learning outcome does not depend on the political application. Run it on commercial material. The method is the thing being taught, and it transfers without being announced.

When a student says their learning app uses the same mechanic. This comes up in week one, every time, and it is not an objection to be deflected. The answer is that the mechanic is not the unit of judgement — the consent is. A streak serving a habit someone chose supports their own intention; the identical streak serving a subscription they are trying to cancel extracts from them. Same code, opposite direction of agency. Three questions settle it: does the person hold the goal or did the system supply it, would the mechanic still work if they could see exactly how it was built to affect them, and does leaving cost them nothing. This course is about the case where all three fail, which is most commercial deployment. The third course of this programme is about the case where they can be made to hold, and it is where a student who raises this should be sent.

Keep your own numbers. Run the Restoration Delta on your own sessions, in both directions, and publish the results to the cohort including the sessions that deplete them. A facilitator who has never shown the room a bad reading of their own teaching has not actually taught the instrument.
